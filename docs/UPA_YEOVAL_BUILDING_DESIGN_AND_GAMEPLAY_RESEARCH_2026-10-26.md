# Yeoval UPA aged-care building design: evidence review and 3D correction brief

**Research date:** 26 October 2026. **Location:** 3–7 Lord Street, Yeoval NSW.  
**Status:** Evidence-based design brief, NOT an as-built floor plan or compliance certification.  
**Observed gameplay defect:** User's Android screenshots of preview version `e935bb0` show the smaller, detached-looking brick building with windows but no visible external door. The main long wing has a visible covered entrance. The smaller structure must not be assumed to have its entrance on the photographed elevation; investigate all sides and the covered internal connection before inventing a door.

## Highest-value discovery: the two buildings were physically joined

An account by Yeoval's care manager, Danny Oakenfull, reports that major renovations **joined two separate buildings**, demolished/replaced some rooms and completely redid the dining room. This independently corroborates the 2012 UPA plan to join buildings and the 2016 annual report describing a **covered walkway**. A reconstruction showing two disconnected, unenterable buildings is therefore historically misleading, even if one external wall legitimately lacks a door.

- Primary historic account: https://www.qpsbenchmarking.com/news/upa-yeoval-gaining-a-competitive-advantage-by-increasing-resident-satisfaction/
- UPA 2012 annual report: https://www.upa.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Annual-Report-2012.pdf
- UPA 2016 annual report: https://www.upa.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Annual-Report-2016.pdf

**Implementation priority P0:** visually and physically model a continuous, usable covered connecting route between both residential wings, including a traversable opening at each end. Use an accessible public entrance only where photographic or plan evidence supports its location. If no external entrance is evidenced for the smaller wing, connect it internally instead and label the exterior doorway location **unverified**. Validate gameplay on Android and desktop, including exit/return position, collisions, and unobstructed circulation.

## Specific UPA Yeoval program — not a generic nursing-home template

UPA's publicly available residential-care entry information describes **25 residents** with single rooms and private ensuites; some current UPA and My Aged Care marketing also advertises shared double rooms with ensuites. The 25 figure is places/capacity, **not 25 verified bedrooms**. Historic 2017 government records separate **17 Hazelholme Hostel** and **10 Yeoval Community Nursing Home** places; two places transferred to Bracken House in 2018. Avoid conflating historic capacities or assigning a specific resident population to either 3D wing.

The UPA guide specifically lists:
- **Two large lounges**, including the larger **MAC room** used for activities and special functions
- **A dining area**, **two activities areas**, a **foyer**, and outdoor areas
- **On-site purpose-built kitchen**, preparing meals with resident input
- **Raised garden beds** and comfortable outdoor seating
- Renovated bedrooms with **high/low beds, built-in wardrobes, bedside tables, lockable drawers, reverse-cycle air conditioning, TV/telephone connections and emergency-call buttons**
- Visiting hairdresser, podiatry and physiotherapy services

Source: https://www.upa.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/General-Information-for-Entry-into-Residential-Care.pdf

## Australian aged-care design principles relevant to visual and gameplay quality

The Australian Government **National Aged Care Design Principles and Guidelines** (introduced 1 July 2024) use four themes: *Enable the person, Cultivate a home, Access the outdoors, Connect with community*. Design should feel domestic, legible, socially connected and navigable rather than an institutional blank corridor. Wayfinding should use daylight, visual landmarks, low-glare finishes, clear destinations, seating/resting opportunities, and distinctive room cues. The Australasian Health Facility Guidelines' multipurpose-service planning guide discourages long featureless dead-end corridors.

Sources:
- https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/improving-accommodation-in-residential-aged-care
- https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-08/national-aged-care-design-principles-and-guidelines_0.pdf
- https://healthfacilityguidelines.com.au/sites/default/files/HPU_B.0350_7.pdf

The **strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards** commenced **1 November 2025**. Standard 4 covers safe, comfortable, supportive physical environments, appropriate equipment and infection prevention; Standard 6 addresses food and dining; Standard 7 covers residential community and daily life. These are obligations for providers, not proof that the current Yeoval building has any particular measured corridor or doorway.
- https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/providers/quality-standards/standard-4-environment
- https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/providers/quality-standards/strengthened-aged-care-quality-standards

## Specific outstanding architectural unknowns

1. **Actual external door position(s) on the smaller structure**: no verified elevation drawing or all-sides photographic survey in this research.
2. **Exact junction of the two wings** and whether the connector is glazed, roofed, enclosed or later modified.
3. **Measured footprints, roof profiles and elevations**: existing game dimensions are approximations and cannot be certified.
4. **Which wing contains the MAC lounge, dining, kitchen, activities areas and resident rooms**.
5. **Whether Netherwood reopened** after proposals in 2018–2022.
6. **Current co-located community health entrance**, distinct from resident access.
