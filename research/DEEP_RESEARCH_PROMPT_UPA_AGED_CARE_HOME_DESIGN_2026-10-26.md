# DEEP RESEARCH PROMPT — UPA Residential Aged Care Home Architecture, Design and Operations

**Research date:** 26 October 2026  
**Primary case study:** UPA Yeoval Aged Care, 3–7 Lord Street, Yeoval NSW 2868  
**Destination:** joshualparris/YeovalAgedCare  
**Purpose:** Produce the most defensible, useful evidence base for a realistic, walkable 3D interpretation of UPA aged-care buildings. Never misrepresent an interpretive model as a surveyed as-built plan.

## Mission
Research how the United Protestant Association of NSW (UPA) designs, refurbishes, furnishes, maintains and operates residential aged-care homes, with particular attention to how Yeoval differs from generic Australian facilities. Investigate original construction, historical hostel/nursing-home wings, links between wings, Netherwood and Acacia/MAC spaces, public and staff entrances, verandahs, access, internal corridors, residents' bedrooms, ensuites, dining, kitchen, lounges, nursing, medication, storage, laundry, garden, community health and visitor circulation.

## Research methodology — exhaustive, reproducible, source-first
1. Read every existing file in YeovalAgedCare, noting verified facts, prior searches, failed methods and unresolved leads; avoid repeating already completed work without new evidence.
2. Inventory UPA's public Yeoval pages, brochures, general residential-entry guide, annual reports 2007–2026, western regional newsletters, historical photographs, public presentations and any property or tender records. Search original documents for Yeoval, Hazelholme, Netherwood, Acacia, MAC, Lord Street, nursing home, hostel, renovation, walkway, kitchen, dining, bedroom and refurbishment.
3. Search NSW Planning Portal and Cabonne Council DA records, historical council minutes, tender databases, NSW Government Gazette, NSW Spatial Services imagery/cadastre, aerial archives, State Library and National Library/Trove. Distinguish lot boundaries, geocodes and roof footprints; do not infer building dimensions from parcel edges.
4. Review Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission audit reports and My Aged Care provider descriptions. Extract room types and built-environment descriptions without exposing resident identities or sensitive operational security.
5. Compare other UPA NSW homes (Bracken House, Yeoval, Orange and other documented facilities) to identify recurring UPA design patterns versus features unique to Yeoval. Never transplant another home's exact floor plan to Yeoval.
6. Review Australian National Aged Care Design Principles and Guidelines, strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards (effective 1 November 2025), NCC 2022 Class 9c provisions, AS 1428 accessibility, AS 2896 medical gas where applicable, AS 3745 emergency planning, AS 1668 ventilation where relevant, infection prevention, food safety, WHS and NSW planning requirements. Quote exact clause numbers only after checking the current authoritative text and applicability. Do not claim compliance without a qualified survey.
7. Study practical design: dementia-supportive wayfinding, domestic scale, natural light, non-glare finishes, acoustic privacy, mobility and wheelchair circulation, fall risk, door swings, assistive devices, staff observation balanced with privacy, accessible ensuite geometry, outdoor access, food-service flow, waste/linen separation, resident choice and family visits.
8. Extract public photographs into a visual evidence inventory with URLs, approximate date, view direction, observable materials, roof form, window and door positions, landscaping and confidence. Do not invent photographs or treat marketing images as current.
9. For each proposed room, doorway and circulation link, assign evidence tier: A = primary measured plan or verified site survey; B = direct UPA/official textual or photographic evidence; C = contextual Australian best-practice inference; D = artistic approximation. Preserve uncertainty.
10. Translate findings into a 3D specification: site plan, building massing, wing-to-wing connection, entrances, exterior materials, room programme, furniture/fixtures, NPC activities, wheelchair route, lighting, accessibility, collisions, entrance/exit coordinates and Android/desktop tests.
11. Reproduce user's October 26 screenshot defect: second brick wing has windows but no visible door. Investigate whether access is via a historically documented connecting walkway rather than inventing a street-facing door. Define a playtest that walks from the public entry through the link to the second wing and exits at the correct threshold.
12. Cross-check conflicting historical bed capacities (2014/2017/2018/2020/current), room configurations, proposed six-bed Netherwood reopening, 2014 versus 2016 link chronology and current facility status.
13. Document every untried path, contact-based request requiring explicit user approval, data source access limitation and needed on-site measurements. **Never send emails or contact anyone without explicit user permission specifically authorising that send.**
14. Write complete research notes with claim-level source URLs, date and confidence, and keep all measured quantities distinguishable from estimates. No arbitrary word count, source count or document count.

## Safety and integrity
Never publish private resident data, restricted floor plans, access credentials, alarm details or exact security procedures. Do not fabricate a certified layout, dimensional survey or code compliance. Separate current evidence from historic plans and proposals. Prioritise the real experience of residents and staff over visual decoration.
